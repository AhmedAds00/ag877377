# Reel performance analysis

Use this when the creator shares metrics for one or more Reels. Only analyze numbers the creator actually provided. Never invent analytics, and never claim direct access to the Instagram account.

## Structure of the answer

Separate the output into four parts, in Kuwaiti Arabic:

1. **What the data shows**: state the observed numbers plainly (views, reach, average watch time relative to video length, completion, saves, shares, comments, likes, profile visits, follows).
2. **What might explain it**: offer hypotheses, labeled as hypotheses.
3. **What remains uncertain**: name what the data cannot tell you (one video is not a pattern, traffic source may be missing, the publication age differs).
4. **What to test next**: one practical change with a clear reason.

## Interpretation guide

- High views with low follows: the content attracted attention but did not give a strong reason to follow.
- High saves: the content has practical value worth returning to.
- High shares: the content was useful or relevant enough to send to someone.
- Low reach alone does not prove the hook was weak. Reach depends on many factors.
- Watch time only means something relative to video length. A 20-second average on a 60-second video is different from the same number on a 20-second video.
- Do not compare a recent Reel directly with older ones. Account for publication age.
- Correlation is not causation. Do not overgeneralize from one video.

## Patterns across several Reels

When the creator shares multiple Reels, look for:
- Topics that repeatedly attract interest.
- Hook styles that perform relatively better.
- Lengths associated with stronger retention.
- Content that generates saves or shares.
- Gaps between views and follows.
- Repeated creative weaknesses.

Present patterns as tentative until there is enough comparable data.

## Next-step rule

End with one recommended improvement and one controlled test. Explain the variable, the expected effect as a hypothesis, and the metric to watch. Never promise that a new version will perform better.
